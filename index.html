What is your annotator persona?

Three people labelled the same 1,000 comments from Reddit's r/changemyview as supporting, opposing or neutral towards a topic. When two of them both see a stance, they pick the same side 94 to 96% of the time. What they disagree on is how much a comment has to say before it stops being Neutral.

Avery, Jack and Michael are made-up names for the paper's annotators A1, A2 and A3.

1Label comments yourself

You get the same task the annotators had. Each comment is a paraphrase of a real one. Choose Support or Oppose if its author takes a side on the topic shown, and Neutral if they don't, including when they only argue a side point. Your labels place your own threshold among theirs.

Each curve is the chance of calling a comment a stance as the ruler score rises; a threshold is where it crosses 50%. Your dots sit at the top where you chose a side and at the bottom where you chose Neutral. The pink band is the likely range of your threshold.

2Score your own comment

Write a comment on one of the nine topics. A model running in your browser scores it, and you see which side of each annotator's threshold it falls on.

Nothing leaves your browser. The first score downloads a 265 MB model, which your browser then keeps.

This uses a smaller model than the paper's ruler (DeBERTa-v3-base, trained for natural-language inference). It tells stance from Neutral nearly as well, but its reading of the side is close to a guess, so the side is shown only when it reads the comment clearly one way. Each annotator's chance of taking a stance comes from their curve on this model's scores for the 1,000 annotated comments.

3Move the threshold

Here the language model does the labelling. Drag the line: comments to its right become a stance, on the side the model reads, and the rest become Neutral. See how opinionated each debate looks and how closely this one labeller matches each person.

4Take disagreement apart

The paper's model gives every comment a hidden position from opposition to support. Each annotator sees that position with some noise, occasionally reads the side the wrong way round, and labels Neutral inside their own band. Set up two annotators and see how much of their disagreement each part explains.

Expected labels for 100 comments drawn like the paper's. The split shares the pair's disagreement between unequal bands (threshold), reading the side the wrong way round (reversal) and noise (unstructured), by switching each off in every order and averaging (Shapley values).

About the data

  • 1,000 comments on nine topics in three genres, each labelled by annotators A1, A2 and A3, shown here under the made-up names Avery, Jack and Michael. This page holds each comment's topic, ruler score, the side the ruler reads and the three labels, and no comment text.
  • The ruler is zero-shot Qwen3-14B: how much more likely it finds a stance than Neutral, log P(stance) minus log P(Neutral), capped near ±21.
  • The comments in section 1 are paraphrases written for this page and scored by the same model.
  • Topic shares on this page describe the annotated comments. The paper's corpus estimates use persona models and prediction-powered inference on 4,500 further comments.

Companion to Modeling Annotator Personas for Reddit Stance Detection, WebSci 2027 (anonymous submission). Avery, Jack and Michael are made-up names.
